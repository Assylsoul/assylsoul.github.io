Things I built

Healthcare fraud detection

Why: Fraud in healthcare insurance is hard to spot in huge piles of claims. I wanted a model that flags risky providers early.

What I did: I analyzed 700,000+ Medicare claims from 5,410 providers (Kaggle), built 24 fraud-signal features and compared five models: Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM and a Stacking Ensemble.

Result: Final Logistic Regression model with ROC-AUC 0.966 and F1 0.710, shipped as an interactive Streamlit app that scores fraud risk in real time.

Python · Scikit-learn · Streamlit

Diabetes progression study

Why: To find out which patient factors really explain how diabetes progresses, and to check that the model can be trusted.

What I did: In R I compared a full model, an EDA-based model and an AIC stepwise model, and ran full diagnostics: VIF, Cook’s Distance, Breusch-Pagan and Shapiro-Wilk.

Result: The stepwise model won with Adjusted R² 0.508 and RMSE 53.63, and every predictor was statistically significant (p < 0.001).

R · Multiple linear regression
